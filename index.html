About

Commercial success is harder than scientific success. A strong technology still has to survive contact with a real market, and that is a different problem from building it.

I am a commercial operator with a scientific background. That market means budget owners, procurement, regulatory requirements such as CE-IVDR and GMP, evidence requirements, adoption risk and long buying cycles. It is where good products often fail, and where most of the value in commercialisation actually lives.

My core expertise is in life sciences: 3D cell culture and organoids, with hands-on experience across genomics, proteomics, diagnostics and the wider research-tools space. The commercial approach travels well beyond that. The same discipline around qualification, evidence and buying behaviour applies to most scientific, engineering and other complex or high-consideration technical solutions.

I work as a fractional GTM operator across the full commercial arc: from laying the foundations (first sales, choosing the right partners and distributors, sorting logistics) through to establishing the commercial processes, qualification and forecasting discipline that make growth repeatable. I can also work hands-on, from lead generation through to closing opportunities. I also founded AlphaSales, a qualification engine that applies the same evidence-based thinking to sales pipelines.

Frameworks

Named methods I have developed and use to make sales qualification and forecasting more rigorous. These are the tools behind my work, and behind the AlphaSales software platform.

Forecast Confidence and Qualification Displacement

Public methodology note, v1.3 · July 2026

In life-science sales, a deal can sound positive while the commercial core remains unresolved. The science may be compelling, the next technical step agreed and the CRM stage advancing, while the budget type, economic buyer, decision path, evidence gate, procurement route or conversion criteria stay unclear. That is how forecast confidence breaks: revenue is marked as committed, but there is not enough qualification evidence to support the number. The purpose of this method is to show which deals can be trusted, which need intervention, and which are still assumption-led, before those numbers reach a board, investors or a financing process.

Qualification displacement

Qualification displacement is the diagnostic mechanism behind many forecast surprises. It occurs when critical buying information (budget confirmation, decision criteria, economic buyer, evaluation gates or process) is not qualified on the call where it should have been captured, and only appears later through follow-up, CRM notes, internal review or last-minute deal rescue. The commercial cost shows up in four ways: longer cycle length, deal-size drift, lower win rate and weaker forecast accuracy.

In the AlphaSales engine

The section above is the framework, the way I think about forecast confidence and qualification displacement. What follows is how the AlphaSales software puts it to work: what the engine actually measures in a sales-call transcript.

What the engine measures: four layers

Layer 1 · Qualification depth

How thoroughly the rep established the conditions for a clean close, scored against nine dimensions: budget, authority, need, timeframes, account context, competition, risks, demo or pilot, and process. Calibrated to call type and deal stage.

Layer 2 · Commercial risk patterns

Deal conditions that can stall progress even when qualification is complete: funding instability, procurement constraints, hidden blockers, unscoped evidence requirements or switching cost without a mitigation plan. Score and risk are kept analytically separate.

Layer 3 · Commercial intelligence beyond the deal

Signals wider commercial functions need to see: KOL opportunities, recurring competitor mentions, buyer events, publication or collaboration openings, voice-of-customer candidates and referral routes. Single signals are tracked; recurring ones are surfaced with a count.

Layer 4 · Predictive outcome correlation

How qualification patterns map onto cycle length, deal-size evolution, win rate and forecast accuracy. This is what separates a qualification audit from conversation intelligence: it measures whether deals will close cleanly, not whether the conversation happened.

Mapped, not scored

Two elements are mapped rather than scored, because a single number would lose what makes them useful. Buyer decision path reconstructs how the specific buyer reaches a decision: who has to agree, what must be built or approved, where deals of this type tend to stall, and how far the current opportunity has moved compared with a typical purchase. Regulatory exposure flags where market access, conformity, quality-system, records-integrity or data-handling requirements appear commercially relevant, whether they were addressed or left open. This is commercial risk, not compliance advice.

Scope, and what it does not measure

The current approach uses sales-call transcripts only, working downstream of recording tools such as Gong, Zoom, Teams, Otter, Fireflies or Granola. It deliberately does not measure talk time, sentiment, monologue length, likeability or whether a meeting hit its agenda. Those are conversation-intelligence outputs. This is the diagnostic layer above them: it tells a commercial leader which deals carry hidden qualification gaps or structural risk that call summaries cannot see.
